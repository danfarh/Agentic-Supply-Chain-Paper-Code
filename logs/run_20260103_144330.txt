There may be a misalignment of interests, where companies prioritize their own benefits over those of the stakeholders.

2. **Information Asymmetry**: Companies may have more information about their practices than the stakeholders. This can lead to biased reporting, where companies may present themselves more favorably than they actually are, affecting the accuracy of the Scoring sheet.

3. **Incentives and Accountability**: If companies are rewarded for high scores without proper verification, they may engage in "score inflation" or "greenwashing," where they exaggerate their ethical practices to improve their scores. This can create a bias in the data, as the scores may not reflect true practices.

4. **Regional Bias**: Different regions may have varying standards and expectations regarding ethical practices. If the scoring criteria do not account for these regional differences, it may lead to biased evaluations that do not accurately reflect the local context.

5. **Selection Bias**: If the companies included in the Scoring sheet are not representative of the entire industry, this can lead to biased conclusions. For example, if only larger companies with better resources are included, the scores may not reflect the practices of smaller companies.

6. **Measurement Bias**: The indicators used to score companies may not capture the full scope of ethical practices. If certain important aspects are overlooked or inadequately measured, this can lead to biased scores.
